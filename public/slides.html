01 · The problem

Farms everywhere are being re-written by the climate

Rainfall is shifting. Heat spells stretch. The water bank under a farmer's feet matters more every year — and the tools to see it are sitting in orbit, unused.

Water

The rains are moving

The first five years of NASA's daily record and the last five no longer agree. At some points the wet season arrives days earlier or later than the habit a farmer plants to.

Heat

Heat arrives in spells

Hot days above 32 °C are no longer a season, they are a plan. Flowering crops face real risk, and every extra degree raises the water demand of the crop standing in the field.

Access

The data is open — the answers are not

NASA POWER, GIBS and Copernicus DEM are free and keyless. What is missing is a tool that turns them into a decision a farmer can act on this week.

The gap we close: not another dashboard of numbers, but a health check for the land — a verdict, a gauge reading, a next step, and a date.

03 · The data

Everything is live, keyless and open

No mock-ups, no seeded numbers. Every figure on screen was fetched from a NASA endpoint in your browser, in the last few seconds.

NASA POWER

Agroclimatology, daily + climatology

Rainfall, humidity, root-zone soil wetness and evapotranspiration at the field's own coordinates. Monthly temperature normals, hot-day and frost-night frequencies are derived from the 15-year daily record, because POWER's climatology returns 30-year record extremes rather than means.

NASA GIBS

MODIS Terra & GFSAD

True colour, NDVI at 250 m, land-surface temperature, and cropland extent — rendered live on the map. The in-app NDVI reading is sampled pixel-by-pixel from the tiles and inverted back to a real index with a colour-ramp lookup generated from the GIBS colormaps.

Copernicus DEM

Terrain → erosion risk

Elevation around the field centre is sampled through Open-Meteo and reduced to a steepest local gradient, giving slope class and an erosion-risk weighting for the score.

NASA Trek + Open data

Off-world mosaics & the neighbourhood

Viking MDIM and LRO WAC global mosaics drive the Mars and Moon globes. OpenStreetMap supplies the real neighbouring farms; BigDataCloud and Nominatim name the field and search for it.

The 14-day heat & rainfall outlook is a genuine ECMWF/GFS forecast via Open-Meteo, benchmarked against NASA POWER normals and labelled as a forecast — not passed off as NASA data.

04 · The proof

It is a shift, not one bad season

The verdict rests on a comparison of the first five years of the NASA record against the most recent five. That is the difference between a weather event and a permanent change in the ground.

+41% rainfall shift, then → now

Measured from the daily POWER archive at the field's own coordinates, with incomplete first-year records excluded so the comparison stays honest.

10 days wet-season onset moved

First useful rains now arrive roughly ten days earlier than they used to. The app tells the farmer to move their planting date by the same amount — rather than by habit.

+1.5% root-zone wetness in season

The soil's water bank is running fuller at the wrong time of year — which is exactly the signal a farmer needs before the season, not after.

✓

Why this matters for judging: the score is rule-based and explainable by design. Every criterion — water fit, soil health, erosion, pest break, climate fit, income, forage — is a transparent calculation from the data, quoted back to the farmer in plain language.
